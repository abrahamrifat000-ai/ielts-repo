Proof of funds

Bank statement, solvency and maturity

Money is the most common reason for a refusal. These are the terms embassies use, followed by what each of the 35 countries asks for.

Bank statement

The transaction history of an account, usually for the last three to six months, printed and stamped by the bank. It shows the balance and how it got there.

Solvency certificate

A one-page letter from the bank stating the balance on a given date, often with a foreign-currency equivalent. It supports a statement; few countries accept it alone.

Maturity (holding period)

How long the money must sit in the account before you apply. The UK sets 28 days exactly; many others expect three to six months of steady history.

Blocked account and GIC

Money you transfer in advance to a bank in the destination country, released monthly after you arrive. Germany, Belgium and Canada use this model.

Sponsor's affidavit

A notarised promise from a parent or close relative to pay for your studies, backed by their bank statement, income proof and tax papers.

Source of funds

Evidence of where a large deposit came from: salary, business income, a land sale, a gift deed or a bank loan. Unexplained lump sums are treated as borrowed money.

Twelve months to departure

A timeline that works for most countries

Count back from your intake. For a September start, begin the September before.

  1. 12 to 10 months beforePrepare for and sit IELTS. Shortlist countries with the finder, then five to eight universities.
  2. 10 to 8 monthsCollect transcripts, write your statement of purpose, ask for recommendation letters and submit applications.
  3. 8 to 6 monthsReceive offers. Apply for scholarships; most deadlines fall in this window.
  4. 6 to 4 monthsPlace the money in the right account so it reaches maturity. Get a police clearance and start medical tests.
  5. 4 to 2 monthsAccept the offer, pay the deposit, then apply for the visa and attend biometrics or the interview.
  6. 2 months to departureBook housing and flights, buy insurance, and make certified copies of every document.
